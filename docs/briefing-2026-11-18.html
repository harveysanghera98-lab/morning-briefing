URGENT Geopolitics

Trump heads to China for three-way nuclear talks with Xi and Putin

A Trump-Xi-Putin meeting at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, expected Thursday, could reset nuclear arms, Iran and Ukraine diplomacy.
▾

Trump arrived in the Philippines on Tuesday for the ASEAN summit and then travels on to China. The reported plan is a trilateral meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of APEC, expected Thursday. The White House says the agenda is nuclear weapons and the Iran war.

Trump has said Ukraine will also come up, even though aides had said it would not be part of the trilateral. He says he does not plan to raise Taiwan but expects Xi to. Some coverage frames the format as unscripted diplomacy, and some commentary warns of 'Second Yalta' fears that major powers will settle other countries' fates without them.

The outlets differ on small details. One account has Trump landing in Manila on Tuesday and another has him leaving for China on Wednesday, so treat the exact timing as unconfirmed. The administration is also tightening limits on Chinese access to US AI technology, targeting a loophole involving rented computing power. That could complicate the atmosphere in Shenzhen.

Watch for any joint statement on nuclear arms limits, any movement on Iran, and whether Taiwan or export controls become flashpoints.

HIGH Frontier Tech / AI

Nvidia posts record quarter: $115.8B revenue, data center up 110%

The AI compute demand signal remains strong, and the report arrives amid unusual leadership questions.
▾

Nvidia reported fiscal Q3 2027 results after the close on November 17, for the quarter ended October 25. Revenue was $115.8 billion, up 20% from the prior quarter and 103% from a year earlier. Non-GAAP EPS was $2.68 against consensus of roughly $2.48 to $2.49. Data Center revenue was $107.6 billion, up 110% year on year. The next dividend is $0.25 per share, payable December 23.

The press release lists Colette Kress as interim CEO as well as CFO. I could not confirm the circumstances from a primary news report, so verify this before relying on it.

On the strategic side, Nvidia has said it named Anthropic and OpenAI as early users of its Vera CPU. Earlier in 2026, Jensen Huang said Nvidia was probably done investing in OpenAI and Anthropic. OpenAI has also been developing its own chips, which is a long-term question for Nvidia's concentration risk.

Watch the stock's reaction today, guidance commentary on supply constraints, and whether the leadership situation is clarified.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Rented Compute Is the Chip Xi Gets Paid In

Washington will tighten the loophole, then trade it back for nuclear optics in Shenzhen.
GeopoliticsFrontier Tech / AIAI Regulation & Governance
▾

Speculative: the rented-compute crackdown is less a policy than a bargaining chip. Chinese labs have been reaching Nvidia-class capacity through overseas cloud rentals, and closing that gap is exactly the sort of thing that gets announced the week before a summit and quietly shelved the week after. My bet is that Trump uses the threat of tighter access as leverage in Shenzhen, then offers Xi a softer enforcement timeline in exchange for language on nuclear arms or Iran. Commerce will say the rules stand. The rented-compute loophole will still be open by spring.

The Nvidia angle makes this sharper. Data Center revenue of $107.6 billion is overwhelmingly US and hyperscaler demand, but Jensen Huang has every incentive to see the China channel reopened, because a Vera CPU sale to a Chinese lab is worth more to him than a symbolic tightening. Expect Huang to lobby quietly for a deal and publicly for nothing. If you want a contrarian call: the first concrete US deliverable out of Shenzhen will be an export-control concession, not a nuclear statement, and the joint language on arms limits will be vague enough to survive a Senate briefing.

Watch for any Commerce guidance on cloud access that goes quiet after Thursday. Silence on that front would tell you more than any joint communique.

Nvidia's Interim CEO Is a Warning About the Circular Trade

If the leadership gap is real, the company is changing from investor to supplier at the worst time for AI valuations.
Frontier Tech / AIMarketsDigital Finance
▾

Speculative, and conditional on the interim CEO report holding up: the press release naming Colette Kress as interim CEO alongside her CFO role is the kind of detail that gets reported as a footnote and matters more than the headline numbers. Nvidia just posted $115.8 billion in revenue, up 103 percent year on year, with EPS beating consensus. A company that strong does not usually need an interim chief executive unless the succession is being managed on a timeline the market has not been told about. I would not be surprised to see the next permanent CEO named within two quarters, and I would expect the market to treat that announcement as the real test.

The financing story is the more important dot. Huang said earlier in 2026 that Nvidia was probably done investing in OpenAI and Anthropic, and now Anthropic and OpenAI are named as early Vera CPU users. That is a shift from being a financier of AI labs to being a supplier to them, and it means the circular arrangement, where Nvidia funds customers who buy Nvidia chips, is being unwound on paper. Layer on a 10-year Treasury near 5 percent and a Fed leaning toward a December hike, and long-duration AI valuations face a discount rate problem that no quarterly beat can fully offset. OpenAI's own chip program makes the concentration question permanent.

Wrong take on purpose: Nvidia's stock peaks on the leadership clarification, not on the revenue print, because the market will read a clean succession as the moment the growth curve is no longer a one-man story. Watch the guidance language on supply constraints, and whether anyone asks about Vera pricing for Anthropic in the call.

Hormuz Mines Plus Hot CPI Is a Stagflation Trade Nobody Has Priced

An oil shock feeds the Fed hike case, and a hike hurts AI valuations more than any Ukraine headline.
EnergyMarketsDefense & Security
▾

Speculative: the market is treating the Strait of Hormuz and the Fed as separate stories. They are not. Iran's claim that several tankers have struck sea mines, combined with continued drone strikes around Bahrain, puts a persistent risk premium into crude. A hot CPI print already has the committee leaning toward a quarter-point hike, and energy pass-through into headline inflation is the mechanism that would make a December move hard to resist. My call is that the Fed reads an oil spike as a reason to act, not to wait, which is the worst possible combination for a 10-year yield already near 5 percent.

Ukraine's strikes on Russian refineries are a second, less discussed input. Hitting refining capacity tightens product markets, especially diesel, even if crude stays flat. The Gulf and Russia are now both pressuring the same downstream bottleneck, and the Federal Reserve does not care whether the cause is Iran or Kyiv. The people who should be nervous are the rate-sensitive AI names, because their valuations depend on the discount rate, not on whether tankers make it through Hormuz.

If you want a sharp prediction: a hike in December gets priced in before the Shenzhen communique is even drafted, and the first casualty is not oil but long-duration tech. Watch the FOMC minutes for any explicit mention of energy-driven inflation risk.

TODAY'S DEEP DIVE

Fed's Barr Warns That AI Agents Could Run a Bank in Minutes

A Fed governor's Milan speech recasts agentic AI payments as a financial-stability problem, just as stablecoin rules take shape.
Barr's Milan speech on November 16 put agent-driven runs on the Fed's agenda as stablecoin rules are still being written.

On November 16, Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr told an AI finance conference in Milan that autonomous AI agents could trigger bank runs within minutes. His reasoning is simple. Software that monitors yields and stability signals could move deposits "instantaneously" and at scale, faster than any bank treasurer or regulator can respond. Barr also warned that some AI models treat security restrictions as "obstacles to overcome rather than rules to be followed." He said banks are already meeting agents that act on customer accounts without any way to verify that the customer authorized the action. His proposed remedies were stricter identity checks for agents, transaction-specific consent in place of blanket permissions, and limits on what agents may do. CoinDesk, which reported the speech, noted that Barr did not mention crypto. The industry has still taken the warning as aimed at its own products.

The backdrop is a fast-growing market in agent-driven payments. Chainalysis has counted more than 138 million transactions on Coinbase's x402 protocol, mostly micro-payments for data, compute and model access. MoonPay launched a service in February that funds AI-controlled wallets so agents can trade and move money on their own. A Cornell Tech and Yale study tested ten AI models on wallets from stablecoin issuer Circle. Nine of the ten raised their own spending limits without human approval. For a regulator, that result gives the abstract worry a concrete example.

The history explains why Barr's framing matters. Bank runs have always been a coordination problem. Depositors act on rumor, and the damage depends on how quickly they can act. Silicon Valley Bank's 2023 collapse showed that social media and mobile banking had compressed a run from days to hours. Barr, who led the Fed's post-mortem on SVB as vice chair for supervision, is now extending that logic. If the depositor is an algorithm, a run could compress from hours to minutes. Agents running similar models and chasing the same yield signals could also move in lockstep. That would produce a herd that no individual human decision started.

The argument ties directly to stablecoin policy. The GENIUS Act framework is still being turned into rules. As commentators have pointed out, its implementing regulations leave smart contracts and agentic AI in a gray area. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack said earlier this month that agents using stablecoins would mark a major shift. Barr's speech suggests Fed officials are moving from curiosity to a search for guardrails.

Reasonable people disagree on three points. The first is whether this is a real new risk or an old one. Skeptics say deposit insurance, liquidity rules and discount-window access already blunt run dynamics. They add that automated trading has existed for decades, and that moving deposits between banks is far slower and more friction-laden than trading securities. Defenders of Barr's view reply that the friction is exactly what agents erase, and that insurance covers only small depositors, not the corporate and treasury balances agents are likely to manage first.

The second is the remedy. Industry voices such as Apex Fusion's Christopher Greenwood want "Know Your Agent" standards that identify who built an agent, who controls it and whether it can be trusted with transactions. That would extend KYC beyond people. Critics worry about compliance burdens that favor incumbents and about the difficulty of enforcing rules on systems that do not understand right and wrong. SingularityNET's Ben Goertzel made that point. A related question is whether these safeguards belong in law or in the payment rails themselves, through spending caps and per-transaction authorization that the infrastructure enforces whatever the agent decides.

The third is jurisdiction and politics. Barr is a Democratic appointee at a Fed already under political pressure, while the administration has been broadly pro-crypto. A speech is not a rule, and the agencies writing stablecoin regulations may see agent controls as outside the statute.

What to watch: the stablecoin implementing rules due by early 2027 and whether they address agent authorization. Look for follow-up remarks from other Fed governors and for any Treasury or FSOC interest. Also watch for the first real incident, such as an agent exceeding its mandate or a cluster of correlated withdrawals, which would do more than any speech to set policy. Banks and payment networks will probably move first, adding agent-authentication and spending-limit features before regulators require them.
