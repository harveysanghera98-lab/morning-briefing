URGENT Geopolitics

Hormuz mine strikes complicate Iran's reopening offer

Mine attacks on tankers and a US blockade keep the Iran war, and its effect on oil and inflation, unresolved.
▾

Iran's foreign minister says Tehran's seven-day offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz is still on the table, and Iran is reviewing US comments on it. At the same time, the Revolutionary Guards report that tankers hit naval mines in the strait. One supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels caught fire. UKMTO counted 10 shipping attacks in the week ending Friday, and one daily digest calls that the fastest pace since March.

Crude flows through the strait have largely returned to prewar levels, helped by US military escorts. Refined fuel shipments remain well below prewar volumes (CNBC, Sept 30). CENTCOM says its naval blockade of Iran has prevented at least $25 billion in crude sales.

The stakes are economic as well as military. Energy prices are the main driver of US inflation, which has pushed the Fed toward rate hikes. Reports differ on how close the two sides are to a deal. Iranian statements stress conditions, while US messaging stresses pressure. The mine incidents raise the chance that a diplomatic opening stalls.

Watch for a US reply to the seven-day offer, further mine or tanker incidents, and the effect on crude prices ahead of the Fed meeting next week.

HIGH Markets

Hot CPI and tech rout hit stocks; Fed meets next week

Inflation of 3.7% and 10-year yields near 5.1% collide with a heavy Big Tech earnings week.
▾

September CPI rose 3.7% year over year, above the 3.6% forecast, with core at 2.6%. Soaring energy prices from the Iran war are the main driver. The Nasdaq fell more than 4% last week and the S&P 500 about 2%, its worst week since June.

Treasuries rallied during the tech sell-off, with the 10-year yield falling to roughly 5.09% (CNBC, Oct 16). The yield had touched a 24-year high of 5.35% earlier in October. Sources differ somewhat on the exact level.

Markets put the odds of a 25bp hike at the October 27-28 FOMC meeting at about 17%, down from 22% before the CPI report. Odds of a December hike rose. The Fed is in its blackout period, and Chair Kevin Warsh has not signaled his intentions.

This week's earnings from Tesla, Meta and SpaceX are the main test of whether the AI-linked sell-off continues. Also watch the Treasury auctions and Friday's flash PMIs.

HIGH Frontier Tech / AI

Anthropic builds in-house chip team as AI labs chase custom silicon

Compute scarcity is pushing labs to design their own chips and to compete for chip-design talent.
▾

Anthropic confirmed in August that it is building an internal chip-design team for custom processors for Claude. Reuters had first reported in April that it was weighing the plan. The company says it will keep using Nvidia and AMD accelerators alongside its own designs.

Anthropic already relies on Google TPUs, Amazon chips, and a long-term deal with Google and Broadcom. It has also been reported to be in talks with Samsung on a custom chip (TechCrunch, July). OpenAI is working with Broadcom on a custom inference chip, and a lead from its chip program has moved to Anthropic. First custom chips from Anthropic are likely years away.

This is background rather than breaking news. My searches did not return a fresh Oct 19-20 AI story, so treat it as context for this week's tech earnings. Watch what Meta and Tesla say about capex and compute.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

The Fed, Not Tehran, Is the Real Threat to AI Capex

Speculative: a Warsh hike by December hits Nvidia-dependent spending harder than any mine in the strait
GeopoliticsMarketsEnergyFrontier Tech / AI
▾

Speculative. Energy is the transmission belt here. Hormuz mines keep crude and refined fuel expensive, September CPI printed 3.7%, and the 10-year sits near 5.1%. Everyone is watching Iran's seven-day offer, but the variable that reprices multi-year compute contracts is the Fed funds rate. Our bet is that Chair Kevin Warsh, who has signaled nothing, moves from roughly 17% odds of an October hike to a December hike, and that the Anthropic and OpenAI silicon programs get re-underwritten as cash-burn stories rather than moats.

The chain runs like this. Refined shipments lag, so fuel stays tight even if crude flows recover. Headline inflation stays above target, which means the Fed cannot pivot to ease the tech sell-off. Long yields stay pinned near 5%, which raises the discount rate on five-year GPU commitments. Meta, which is guiding to heavy capex, and Tesla, which is running its own AI build-out, get repriced first. SpaceX, if it trades as a growth stock, takes the same hit.

The wrong take: the market is treating a stalled Hormuz deal as a geopolitical risk-off event for tech. We think the dominant channel is rates, so a deal is the bullish path for AI multiples. A real reopening drops crude, eases the inflation print, and gives Warsh room to hold. A stalled deal keeps the inflation impulse alive, and that is worse for AI valuations than any single chip announcement. Watch the December dot plot and the 10-year yield, not the Iranian foreign ministry.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Beijing's Export Rules Will Hit the Chip Talent Market First

Speculative: China's tech-control revision turns engineers and fab access into the new sanctions battlefield
ChinaFrontier Tech / AIApplied Tech & Industry
▾

Speculative. Beijing has proposed export controls on 12 solar-manufacturing technologies inside a broader revision that also touches AI and biotech. The solar part reads as a shot across the bow about manufacturing know-how, not just hardware. The AI part is the one to watch. If China treats process knowledge as a strategic export, then the people who know how to design and fabricate accelerators become the most valuable and most exposed assets in the sector.

Now line that up with the chip-talent moves in the briefing. Anthropic is building an in-house chip-design team, has reportedly been in talks with Samsung on a custom part, and has already hired a lead from OpenAI's custom chip program. Anthropic also depends on Google TPUs, Amazon silicon and a Broadcom partnership. A Korean foundry relationship, a US design team and a Chinese export regime that could reach tooling and talent all sit in the same supply chain. We expect Washington to start treating senior chip-design hires as a national security issue within a year, and Anthropic's Samsung talks as the most exposed node.

The wrong take: the custom-silicon race is not really about performance. It is about jurisdictional risk. The lab that owns its design talent in a jurisdiction Beijing cannot touch will win the next round of compute deals, even if its chips are worse. Nvidia's margin is safe for now. Anthropic's ambitions depend on whether Samsung and the US government see the same risk the way we do.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Washington Needs Russian Barrels More Than It Wants to Punish Moscow

Speculative: the Hormuz refined-fuel gap and a Kyiv-side strike on St Petersburg change the sanctions math
EnergyDefense & SecurityGeopolitics
▾

Speculative. Refined fuel shipments through Hormuz remain well below prewar volumes, even with US escorts moving crude. The CENTCOM blockade has reportedly kept at least $25 billion of Iranian crude off the market. That is a lot of barrels removed from a tight system, and it happens in the same month that Ukraine reportedly set fire to St Petersburg's oil terminal. That strike is an aggregator claim and unconfirmed, so treat it as a hypothesis. If it holds, Moscow's export capacity is damaged at the same moment Gulf product flows are impaired.

Here is the bet. Zelensky says US intelligence sharing has resumed after a pause, which suggests Washington is not willing to let Kyiv's long-range strike campaign stall. But the White House is also staring at fuel prices ahead of a Fed meeting that could turn into a hike. Those two priorities pull in opposite directions on Russian oil. We expect quiet Treasury and State Department tolerance for Russian refined product exports, paired with louder rhetoric about the Kyiv strikes, because a second oil shock would be politically unaffordable in an inflation year.

The wrong take: the Ukraine war is becoming an energy-market story, and the Pentagon is not the decision-maker. Expect the energy desk at Treasury to set the terms of Ukraine's strike campaign more than any battlefield update. Watch whether sanctions enforcement on Russian product exports quietly slows in the next two months. If it does, this thesis is right.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

SpaceX Becomes the Mine-Clearance Bet Nobody Priced

Speculative: cheap naval mines in Hormuz create a defense market where SpaceX and Tesla compete on autonomy
Defense & SecurityApplied Tech & IndustryMarkets
▾

Speculative. The Revolutionary Guards say tankers hit naval mines in the strait, and UKMTO counted 10 shipping attacks in a single week, the fastest pace since March. Mines are cheap to lay and expensive to clear. Escorting a supertanker with a destroyer is a sustained cost, and so far the US answer has been to escort and blockade. The obvious next move is autonomous mine-hunting: unmanned surface vessels, sensor-fused drones and satellite-linked command networks. That is a market where SpaceX has the communications layer, and where Tesla has real autonomy and battery expertise. Neither is a defense prime today.

We think the Meta and Tesla earnings call this week will be read by the defense community as a signal. If either company talks about autonomy or government contracts in terms that could transfer to maritime uses, the Pentagon's budget office will notice. SpaceX's earnings, which the market is treating as a growth-stock story, could carry a defense contract pipeline that nobody modeled. We expect at least one hyperscaler to quietly hire for maritime autonomy before the end of the year.

The wrong take: the Hormuz crisis is not a threat to the energy system so much as a sales pitch for dual-use tech. The company that wins the mine-clearance contract will be a defense newcomer, not Lockheed or Huntington Ingalls, and the market will not price it until a Pentagon announcement forces the issue. Watch SpaceX's guidance language for the word 'government.'
