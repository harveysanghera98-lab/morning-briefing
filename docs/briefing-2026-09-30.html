HIGH Applied Tech & Industry

Anthropic IPO prospectus leak puts valuation above $2 trillion

A leaked filing shows how much capital the frontier labs need, and how large their losses are, as OpenAI seeks a $1.4T valuation.
▾

A leaked Anthropic IPO prospectus reportedly targets a valuation above $2 trillion. It lists about $518 billion in cloud, compute and infrastructure commitments over the next decade. It also shows 2025 revenue of about $4.6 billion against roughly $8 billion in operating losses. Separate search-result summaries say the IPO is expected in November, with Nvidia in talks to take up to $10 billion of it. I could not confirm that from a primary source.

Bloomberg reportedly says OpenAI is seeking $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion pre-money valuation, with annualized revenue above $40 billion and growth of about 70% since July. Other summaries put the revenue figure higher, at roughly $70 billion, so treat the number as unsettled.

The figures reinforce the debate over whether the AI capex cycle is sustainable. Commitments of this size depend on public markets absorbing very large offerings while long-dated yields are at multi-decade highs. This item comes from an aggregator, not from FT, WSJ or Reuters. Watch for the formal S-1 and for any confirmation from those outlets.

HIGH AI Regulation & Governance

Trump convenes AI CEOs for voluntary frontier safety pledge

The administration is choosing industry self-regulation over binding rules, and courts are separately tightening the rules on training data.
▾

President Trump reportedly brought six AI lab CEOs together to sign a voluntary 'Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities'. It includes internal controls and independent audits but creates no new legal mandates. Separate reports say Google, OpenAI and Anthropic are forming a 'Frontier AI Standards Agency', an industry-run body.

Also reported: a Third Circuit ruling that copying training data is not fair use. If accurate, it is the first major appellate decision on the question. It would raise licensing costs and legal exposure for model developers, whatever the voluntary pledge says.

Critics are likely to argue that audits without enforcement are weak, and that the EU AI Act remains the only binding regime among the major jurisdictions. Watch for the text of the commitment, who conducts the audits, and whether the copyright ruling is appealed to the Supreme Court. These details come from an aggregator and need confirmation from primary outlets.

URGENT Markets

Treasury yields at multi-decade highs after Fed hike

The 30-year yield is at its highest since 2002 and the Fed has raised rates for the first time in about three years, which squeezes valuations and funding for AI.
▾

US 30-year Treasury yields hit their highest level since 2002. The Fed raised its benchmark rate by 25 basis points at its September meeting, and many economists expect another hike this year given sticky inflation and higher oil prices. New York Fed President John Williams suggested the Fed could wait until December, which pushed back against market bets on an October move.

Equities were slightly lower on Tuesday: the Dow fell 0.25%, the S&P 500 0.16% and the Nasdaq 0.09%. WTI settled near $89.38, down about 3.5%, on Strategic Petroleum Reserve releases and hopes of easing between the US and Iran.

Higher long yields raise the cost of capital for the debt-funded AI buildout and for the large IPOs now in prospect. Watch this week's US data, including Friday's jobs report, and Fed speakers for any signal on December. These figures come from search summaries of CNBC, Schwab and others. HIGH Geopolitics

Iran war talks and US-China tariff cuts advance; Taiwan arms package stalled

There are signs of easing in the Gulf and in US-China trade, but a record Taiwan arms sale is on hold.
▾

US and Iranian officials held separate talks through mediators to end the seven-month war. Trump denied offering sanctions relief for nuclear concessions, though a limited waiver was granted for pilgrim flights between Iran and Iraq. Iranian officials are privately pessimistic about a deal before the November midterms. Saudi Arabia resumed exports via its East-West pipeline, which weakens Iran's leverage over the Strait of Hormuz.

Beijing and Washington agreed to pursue tariff cuts on about $60 billion of goods after the Trump-Xi summit. Taiwan's defense minister said existing US arms sales are unchanged, but a record $14 billion package remains stalled. Reports say a Trump-Xi National Archives visit was altered after Chinese objections to one document.

In Ukraine, Russia is continuing near-daily ballistic missile and drone strikes on Kyiv. Source is the FDD overnight brief and search summaries, not the preferred outlets. Watch whether the Taiwan package is formally shelved.

TRACKING US Policy & Power

Congress passes stopgap to avoid a shutdown at fiscal year-end

Funding is extended to December 11, which removes a shutdown risk just before the midterms.
▾

Congress passed a continuing resolution before the September 30 deadline. The House vote was 370-48 and the Senate had passed it earlier. Trump is expected to sign. The measure funds the government through December 11 and blocks an OMB proposed rule that would have let political appointees end grants. Shutdowns earlier in 2026 included a 76-day lapse at DHS that ended in April. The fight resumes in December, in the lame-duck session after the midterms.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

The AI Capex Window Is Slamming Shut

US frontier labs need $2T+ in public markets just as yields hit 20-year highs; Chinese competitors are building independent compute stacks with lower capital intensity.
Applied Tech & IndustryMarketsChina
▾

Anthropic needs $2 trillion in valuation to support $518 billion in capex commitments over a decade, while OpenAI seeks $1.4 trillion with reported 70% growth. But the 30-year Treasury yield just hit its highest level since 2002 and the Fed is hiking rates for the first time in three years. The math breaks if public markets don't absorb these offerings at frothy multiples while long-dated yields are spiking. Speculative: This is a race against the clock. If the IPO window closes—if yields stay high or equities re-rate on macro stress—both labs face financing cliffs that could force down capex or equity dilution. Meanwhile ByteDance holds roughly 20 percent of China's 24GW data-center capacity and DeepSeek is open-sourcing alternatives to CUDA (TileLang) optimized for Huawei Ascend chips. Chinese labs are building for permanence in a closed ecosystem; US labs are gambling that public markets will absorb massive offerings at a yield-curve peak. History suggests that trade ends badly.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Voluntary Commitments Meet Involuntary Copyright Law

Trump's frontier AI pledge has no enforcement, but a Third Circuit ruling that training-data copying isn't fair use may cost AI labs billions in licensing—and is headed to SCOTUS.
AI Regulation & GovernanceApplied Tech & Industry
▾

The Trump administration convened six AI CEOs to sign a voluntary Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities with internal controls and audits but zero new legal mandates. Separately, a Third Circuit court ruled that copying training data is not fair use—the first major appellate decision on the question. Speculative: The commitment is theater. A binding copyright ruling will raise licensing costs for model developers far beyond what voluntary audits can absorb or prevent. If the ruling stands and is appealed to the Supreme Court, the precedent could force retroactive licensing for existing models, which would dwarf the cost of independent audits. The regulatory squeeze isn't coming from Trump's voluntary framework; it's coming from courts reinterpreting IP law. Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google signed a pledge they can't enforce against judicial reality. Expect appeals and lobbying for legislative carve-outs.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

The December Renegotiation Trap

Congress locked midterm politics into the post-election lame duck by extending funding to December 11, giving Trump leverage to reverse Iran, China, and Taiwan deals based on how the midterms go.
GeopoliticsUS Policy & PowerMarkets
▾

A continuing resolution extended funding through December 11, removing shutdown risk before the November midterms and converting the post-election lame duck into a renegotiation window. Trump has already granted a limited sanctions waiver for Iran (pilgrim flights), agreed to tariff cuts on $60 billion of US-China goods, and stalled a record $14 billion Taiwan arms package. Speculative: These aren't final positions—they're provisional. If Trump wins big in midterms, expect aggressive reversals in December: a tighter Iran posture, re-escalation of China tariffs, and a formal shelving of the Taiwan package in exchange for Chinese concessions on tech or geopolitics. If midterms weaken him, the concessions stick and yields fall. The market is pricing stability; it should price the reversal scenario. Oil is down 3.5 percent on Iran easing hopes, but that's a December risk-off in the making.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

China's Compute Moat Is Getting Wider

ByteDance controls 20 percent of China's data-center capacity while Huawei and DeepSeek open-source alternatives to US chip architectures. US labs face a rising cost of capital and chip constraints.
ChinaFrontier Tech / AIApplied Tech & Industry
▾

ByteDance reportedly holds about one-fifth of China's 24GW of data-center capacity. DeepSeek has open-sourced TileLang, a CUDA alternative optimized for Huawei Ascend accelerators, along with tooling and compiler infrastructure. Meanwhile, Anthropic is committed to $518 billion in capex with a higher cost of capital (due to rising yields) and limited optionality on chip architecture—bound to Nvidia, constrained by US export controls on advanced chips to China. Speculative: Chinese AI labs are building for structural independence; US labs are competing on an open market with rising financing costs. Anthropic's capex commitments assume a stable cost of capital and continued access to Nvidia's latest. Both assumptions are breaking. China's vertical integration—massive captive compute, indigenous chip alternatives, open-source tooling—creates a permanent advantage in total cost of ownership. By the time Anthropic's IPO priced, that advantage may be priced in. US frontier labs may have missed the window to out-capex their competition.
