US midterms: voters decide Congress as polls point to Republican losses

Election Day will decide whether Trump keeps unified control of Congress for his last two years in office.
▾

Americans vote today for a new Congress, plus governors and state legislatures. Democrats are trying to take Republican majorities. Polls cited in coverage ahead of the vote suggested Republicans could lose the House and possibly the Senate.

Trump voted early in West Palm Beach, Florida, on October 24. He said he voted a straight Republican ticket. He also accused Democrats of corruption and repeated unfounded claims about 2020 voter fraud, according to Khaleej Times and other outlets. He has long criticized early voting but urged Republicans to use it this cycle, as SCMP noted.

A Democratic House would be able to block legislation and open investigations into the administration. That would change the outlook for the budget, tech and AI legislation, and any war powers fights over Iran. I could not retrieve live results or race-level reporting in this session, so this entry reflects pre-election coverage only.

Watch for House and Senate control calls overnight, and for any challenges to results. Also watch how markets and oil react, because the vote comes while US-Iran tensions and rate-hike bets are both elevated.

Reported US strike on Iran's Qeshm Island lifts Brent above $105

A reported covert strike on the eve of the election raises the risk of renewed US-Iran war and a further energy-driven inflation shock.
▾

Business Standard reported that oil rose after reports of US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island. Brent futures were up about 48 cents, or 0.5%, at $105.25 a barrel in early Asian trade. Details are unconfirmed and sources differ on the timing and nature of the strike. One account describes it as covert.

Earlier coverage, including The National and The Hill, said Trump and the Pentagon were weighing fresh strikes. It also said Trump had signaled that a return to full-scale action would come only after the midterms. High fuel prices are a political liability for Republicans.

The energy shock is feeding the rate outlook. Reports put the probability of a December Fed hike near 73%. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has called inflation concerning. Reporting conflicts on whether the Fed held rates at its most recent meeting, so check the Fed's own statement.

Watch for Iranian retaliation, shipping risk in the Gulf, and whether the administration escalates once the vote is over.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

The Midterm Hangover Is a Lame-Duck War Budget

Speculative: Republicans lose the House, and the real Iran decision moves to the December funding fight.
US Policy & PowerDefense & SecurityGeopolitics
▾

Speculative: the midterms do not stop an Iran escalation. They move it. If the House flips, Democrats gain subpoenas and hearings, but a war powers resolution still has to clear a Republican Senate and survive a veto. That makes the Senate the choke point, and the real lever is the lame-duck appropriations and any supplemental for munitions. Expect the Pentagon to fold Iran-related replenishment into a year-end package, with Trump pressing Speaker Johnson and Majority Leader Thune to avoid a shutdown fight on his watch. Trump has reportedly signaled that full-scale action waits until after November 3. My bold call: the first serious Iran escalation comes within six weeks of the count being certified, and it is paid for with a supplemental that Democrats in the House cannot block, only embarrass. Lockheed Martin, RTX and Northrop Grumman are the names to watch for early contract signals, since primes tend to front-run supplementals by months.

The Qeshm report matters less for its details than for its deniability. A covert, unconfirmed strike gives the White House a way to show force while keeping the official ledger clean through election day. If the administration wants a war it does not have to vote on, expect more of this, with the public story arriving only after the escalation is already priced in.

Caveat: the Qeshm strike is unconfirmed, and I could not verify live results. Treat the causal chain above as a hypothesis, not a forecast.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Warsh Hikes Into a War Premium He Cannot Control

Speculative: a December hike would be a policy error that tightens into an oil shock the Fed did not create.
MarketsEnergyGeopolitics
▾

Speculative: the market's 73 percent December hike odds are a reflex, not a considered view. About a third of the current inflation pressure traces to Brent sitting above 105 on a war premium, and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has a choice between hiking into a supply shock and looking like he is hiking into a headline. My bold take is that the Fed does not hike in December. Warsh will talk tough, the dot plot will show one more hike, and then the data softens as energy demand destruction bites. Traders who are long the hike will get burned, and the dollar index near 102 is more vulnerable than consensus assumes if the Fed signals pause while oil stays elevated.

The second-order story is that the energy shock is a tax on Republicans' midterm position, so a December hike would hand Democrats a clean inflation argument heading into 2028. That is a political reason for the Fed to hesitate even if the models say otherwise, and it is the kind of pressure Warsh will deny having felt.

The most useful watch item is Brent's reaction on the first trading day after the election. A sustained move above 110 with no Iranian retaliation would suggest the market is pricing a multi-month campaign, which would strengthen the hike case and weaken my call. Treat all of this as speculative. The Fed's latest decision is not confirmed in this briefing, so check the primary statement before building a position.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Fossil Gas Is the Quiet Winner of the Iran Shock

Speculative: an oil-driven energy scare pushes AI data-center buildouts toward gas turbines and away from the grid mix they promised.
EnergyFrontier Tech / AIMarkets
▾

Speculative: AI capacity is the largest unannounced buyer of firm power in the US, and an oil shock makes the wrong fuel look attractive in a new way. If Brent stays above 100, gas-fired turbines become the default for hyperscale campuses that need power faster than the grid can deliver. Expect GE Vernova, Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Power to see order books grow faster than the AI capex headlines suggest, and expect the hyperscalers to describe this as a bridge to nuclear while signing more gas than they publicly admit.

The bold part: this is a boon to Henry Hub-linked producers and an accidental subsidy for LNG exporters, even though the oil shock is about crude. Gas stays cheaper than oil on a per-unit energy basis, so any Gulf shipping disruption that tightens LNG pushes global gas prices and US export margins up in tandem. Cheniere and Venture Global would benefit from the same Hormuz risk that is lifting oil.

The contrarian risk is that AI capex itself is priced for a rate environment that is turning hawkish. If the Fed does hike, the financing for data-center debt gets more expensive just as the turbine orders arrive. This is a hypothesis about where the marginal megawatt comes from, not a forecast of any specific company's results.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Qeshm Is a Defense Stock Story Wearing a Election Costume

Speculative: the Pentagon's post-election plans, not the midterm result, will decide who wins the next defense rally.
Defense & SecurityUS Policy & PowerMarkets
▾

Speculative: defense equities tend to rally on the first credible escalation headline, then fade if the budget does not follow. The Qeshm report is the first escalation headline of this cycle, and the market reaction so far is mostly in oil, not in primes. That gap is the trade. If the administration pursues a post-midterm strike campaign, the first beneficiaries will be munitions and interceptor makers, not the platform primes. Think Raytheon's SM-series interceptors, Kongsberg's naval strike missiles and Anduril's low-cost drone work, since a short campaign burns through precision munitions faster than it burns through aircraft.

The political angle is that a Democratic House will fight any large supplemental on oversight grounds, but Republicans in the Senate will push for it as a national security vote that also helps the defense industrial base in their states. That makes the defense budget one of the few areas where a split Congress still produces bipartisan money, which is exactly why the primes will look expensive right after the election and cheap by spring.

The weak link in this thesis is that I cannot confirm the strike or any post-election plan from the briefing. If the administration stays in diplomacy, the munitions bid fades and this call is wrong.
