URGENT US Policy & Power

Democrats win House and Senate in midterm rout

Divided government resets the policy landscape for the final two years of Trump's term, including nominations, appropriations and oversight.
▾

Networks called the House for Democrats on election night, and AP followed early Wednesday. Democrats also flipped the Senate. AP counts, as reported by Forbes, put them at 54 seats to the GOP's 44, with Alaska and Mississippi still too close to call. Reported flips include Kansas, Iowa, Texas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina. Some outlets reported different early tallies, so final numbers may shift.

CBS notes that a Democratic Senate can block Trump's nominees. Coverage framed the result as a rebuke of Trump, who is the first president in 20 years to lose both chambers in a midterm. Expect House committee investigations and a tougher fight over appropriations and tech and AI legislation when the new Congress convenes on January 3, 2027.

Watch for the remaining Senate calls, how the White House responds, and the lame-duck session. That session will cover the budget and any pending nominations.

HIGH Markets

Dow posts biggest gain of the year as markets cheer gridlock

Investors are pricing in less risk of sweeping tax and spending changes, with AI and Musk-linked stocks the exceptions.
▾

On Wednesday the Dow rose about 1,569 points, or 3%, its best post-midterm day since 1982, according to CNN citing CFRA. The S&P 500 gained 1.58% to close near 7,689, the Nasdaq rose 1.44%, and the VIX fell almost 20%.

Analysts cited by CNN and CBS argue that gridlock limits Congress's ability to pass sweeping tax and spending packages, which reduces volatility. Solar companies, dollar stores and hospital operators led the gains. CBS says investors remain wary of the AI sector, Trump's social media company and Elon Musk's businesses.

The backdrop is still tight. Earlier 2026 reporting had the 10-year Treasury yield near multi-year highs, and markets were pricing possible Fed tightening rather than cuts. Watch whether the rally holds as rates and oil data arrive.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Jensen Huang Is Pricing In a Democratic Congress

Nvidia's exit from OpenAI and Anthropic is a hedge against oversight fights, not a vote of no confidence in the labs
Frontier Tech / AIUS Policy & Power
▾

Speculative: Huang's signal that the latest OpenAI and Anthropic investments could be Nvidia's last reads as a calculated retreat, and the timing is the tell. A Democratic Senate can block nominees, and a Democratic House gets subpoena power when the new Congress convenes January 3, 2027. Nvidia does not want its balance sheet tangled with two labs that may face hearings over circular compute financing. The reported $36 billion debt package tied to Google chips is exactly the structure an oversight staff will call circular and then put on a timeline.

My sharp take: Nvidia is not backing away because it lost faith in the labs. It is backing away because it wants to keep selling them chips without owning a piece that makes it a co-defendant. Expect Nvidia to lobby quietly for a light-touch export regime during the lame-duck session while publicly endorsing whatever chip-security bill emerges from the Senate.

The second-order bet: OpenAI and Anthropic will try to file for IPOs before the new House holds its first hearing. If the Senate map is as bad for the labs as the rout suggests, the IPO window narrows fast in early 2027, and a compute-financing story becomes the first thing a Democratic oversight staff reads.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

The Gridlock Rally Is Really a Short Position on AI

Markets are cheering the end of tax risk while quietly betting that AI firms lose their federal cover
MarketsAI Regulation & GovernanceUS Policy & Power
▾

Speculative: a Dow move of about 3%, its best post-midterm day since 1982, led by solar, dollar stores and hospital operators, while AI and Musk-linked names lagged, is not a pure gridlock trade. Investors say gridlock blocks sweeping tax and spending packages. The laggards suggest they are also pricing in the end of the assumption that Washington will write permissive AI rules.

My sharp take: the market thinks divided government protects everyone. It does not. A Democratic House will use investigations to set the agenda for AI safety, data use and Trump Media-style platform oversight, and a Democratic Senate can stall any federal preemption push that the industry wants to ride out state rules. The AI discount in the tape is a bet that the regulatory overhang gets worse, not that revenue slows.

Watch Trump Media, Tesla and any AI name that trades on Musk headlines. If they keep underperforming while the Dow holds its gains, the market is telling you it believes the AI trade needs Washington's blessing, and Washington just took that blessing away.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Solar Is the Only Honest Bet on the Tape

The solar rally is a defensive trade on clean-energy credits surviving the next reconciliation fight, and it ignores rates at its peril
EnergyUS Policy & PowerMarkets
▾

Speculative: gridlock does not obviously help solar. A divided Congress cannot pass new clean-energy credits either. So the bid has to be defensive. The market is betting that a Democratic Senate blocks the next reconciliation package that would claw back production and investment credits, which means the existing regime survives untouched through the end of the decade. That is a sharper read than 'gridlock is good,' and it is the one I believe.

The problem is rates. Solar is a long-duration, rate-sensitive asset, and the 10-year Treasury sitting near multi-year highs with the Fed priced for tightening is a direct headwind. For solar to outperform, the market has to believe the rate pressure is temporary. My wrong-but-bold call: the rates story will break solar's rally before the credits story does.

Watch the lame-duck budget for any energy riders and watch the Treasury and Interior nominees the White House sends to a Democratic Senate. If the first reconciliation attempt in 2027 targets clean-energy credits, solar becomes a short overnight. Until then, it is the cleanest expression of what investors actually fear, which is not taxes but the next Republican tax bill.
