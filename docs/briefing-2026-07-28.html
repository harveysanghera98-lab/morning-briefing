URGENT Markets

Fed Meets as Oil-Driven Inflation Scare Muddies the Rate Call

A decision that looked like an easy hold two weeks ago is now a real toss-up as war-driven oil prices collide with sticky inflation.
▾

The FOMC's two-day meeting concludes Wednesday with Chair Kevin Warsh, who has repeatedly said inflation is "too high" and that he has no tolerance for it, presiding over a committee that is more divided than it looked in June. Headline CPI is running near 4.1% and core near 3.4%, both well above the Fed's 2% target, and nine of nineteen policymakers have signaled they expect at least one more hike this year. Economists still lean toward a hold at 3.50%-3.75%, which would be a fifth straight standpat meeting, but CME FedWatch odds of a surprise hike more than tripled, from about 11% on July 15 to nearly 35% by July 22, after Brent crude spiked past $100 a barrel on Middle East escalation.

The timing compounds the difficulty: newly reinstated tariffs that lapsed Friday are adding fresh cost pressure just as oil-driven shipping, diesel and jet-fuel costs are climbing. Fed officials have grown more open-minded about AI's potential to be disinflationary over time, but that is a multi-year story, not one that resolves the near-term inflation print. Thursday's data dump of Q2 GDP, June PCE, personal income/spending and jobless claims lands one day after the decision, effectively grading the Fed's call in real time.

Coverage differs on emphasis: Kiplinger and Chase frame this as Warsh drawing an inflation-fighting line in the sand, while Yahoo Finance and The Motley Fool stress how unusual it is for markets to be pricing meaningful hike odds this late in a cutting cycle. Watch for the language in Wednesday's statement on tariffs and energy prices, and whether any dissents emerge from hawks on the committee.

URGENT Defense & Security

Houthi Strikes on Saudi Oil Facilities Push Crude Toward $100

A widening Red Sea front in the Iran conflict is rattling the two chokepoints that carry a huge share of the world's oil.
▾

Iran-backed Houthi forces fired missiles and drones at two of Saudi Arabia's most strategically important Red Sea oil facilities and claimed attacks on Saudi tankers, alongside threats to blockade the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a key bypass for the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude jumped 7% to $100.69 a barrel on July 23, its highest level in nearly two months, before retreating to roughly $82.62 by July 27 as attacks paused and diplomacy resumed, according to trading data. Even with the pullback, futures are still up roughly 30% over the past month, and shipping, insurance, diesel and jet-fuel costs are rising faster than crude itself.

President Trump warned of "major military punishment" against Iran if the Houthis target more ships, while Secretary of State Rubio publicly called on the group to de-escalate. UN Secretary-General António Guterres warned the situation is "getting out of control." Al Jazeera frames this explicitly as a new front opening in the broader US-Iran war, while CNN and Britannica emphasize the Strait of Hormuz as the bigger tail risk if the conflict widens further.

This is landing at the worst possible moment for the Fed, which meets this same week, and for global shippers already navigating elevated freight costs. Watch for whether the pause in strikes holds through the week, any OPEC+ response to the price swing, and whether Washington moves from rhetoric to direct military action against Iranian-linked targets.

HIGH Applied Tech & Industry

Microsoft, Meta, Apple, Amazon Earnings Test the AI Capex Story

Wall Street's patience with hyperscaler AI spending is cracking right before the four biggest reports of the quarter land.
▾

Alphabet shares plunged 7% on July 23 after the company raised its 2026 capex guidance to $195-205 billion (from $180-190 billion) and reported negative free cash flow, -$5.9 billion, for the first time since its 2004 IPO, even as cloud revenue jumped 82% to $24.8 billion. CFO Anat Ashkenazi warned 2027 capex will rise "significantly" further, and Morgan Stanley now sees Alphabet's spend climbing as high as $375 billion next year. That result set a tense tone for the rest of the Magnificent Seven reporting this week.

Microsoft and Meta report Wednesday, Apple and Amazon Thursday. Microsoft faces scrutiny over Azure growth guided at 39-40% constant-currency; Meta is expected to post $60.22 billion in revenue against flat year-over-year EPS of $7.14; Amazon's AWS growth, which accelerated to 28% in Q1, needs to clear a roughly $40.5 billion cloud-revenue consensus. Combined Big Tech capex is now pacing above $200 billion annually.

Fortune and Yahoo Finance describe a market in open "revolt" over whether AI infrastructure spending will ever show a clear return, while bulls point to accelerating cloud growth as vindication. The split will likely determine whether this week's reports trigger a broader tech selloff or a relief rally. Watch commentary on 2027 capex plans and any new disclosure of AI revenue attribution.

HIGH Frontier Tech / AI

Nvidia Weighs $250 Billion Backstop for OpenAI's Ohio Megacampus

The size of this financing structure signals private debt markets alone won't underwrite AI's infrastructure buildout anymore.
▾

Nvidia is reportedly in talks to guarantee roughly $250 billion in lease and construction debt so OpenAI can occupy SoftBank's 10-gigawatt "PORTS Technology Campus" being built in Piketon, Ohio, according to reporting cited by Tom's Hardware and Reuters-sourced accounts that outlets caution has not been independently confirmed. Under the structure, if OpenAI can't make lease payments, Nvidia covers the shortfall while OpenAI keeps the building; Nvidia is separately discussing financing roughly $350 billion in chip purchases for the same site. Total project cost, including Nvidia silicon, could eventually exceed $500 billion, with the first 800-megawatt phase targeted for 2028.

TechTimes frames the arrangement as proof that conventional debt markets balked at underwriting AI infrastructure at this scale, forcing the chipmaker itself to absorb the credit risk of its biggest customer. Nvidia separately agreed to buy about $1 billion of new shares in South Korea's Naver, taking a 4.5% stake tied to that company's AI data-center expansion — another example of Nvidia using its balance sheet to lock in downstream demand.

Nothing is finalized, and terms could still change. Watch for confirmation of the deal structure, credit-rating agency commentary on Nvidia's expanding contingent liabilities, and whether other hyperscalers adopt similar vendor-financing arrangements.

TRACKING China

Moonshot's Kimi K3 Becomes the Largest Open-Weight Model Yet

China's push to give away frontier-scale weights for free is reshaping the AI race just as Washington weighs tighter chip export enforcement.
▾

Moonshot AI's Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter model, went live as a free download at 00:00 UTC on July 27 — the largest open-weight release to date. It lands days after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and other US executives publicly backed open-source AI development, a sign of widening strategic divides within the American AI industry over open versus closed models, per Semafor. Chinese open-weight models are reportedly gaining real traction among US developers despite the geopolitical backdrop.

That backdrop includes active enforcement: Taiwanese prosecutors are investigating roughly $22 million of smuggled Nvidia GB300 chips routed into China via 50 server sets, and Singaporean authorities have separately charged four people over similar smuggling. Beijing has taken a notably more aggressive tone toward US sanctions threats on Chinese AI firms even while muting its reaction to newly reinstated US tariffs, according to Semafor — a sign AI has become the sharper edge of superpower economic competition.

This all sets up a loaded few months: Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 release keeps the capability race hot on the US side, and Xi Jinping's confirmed September 24 state visit to Washington — being prepped via a Rubio-Wang Yi meeting in Manila — is expected to put AI cooperation back on the table. Watch for any new US export-control action and adoption data on Kimi K3 relative to US frontier labs.

Warsh's Inflation Theatre Greenlights Trump's Oil Diplomacy

Fed rate-hike odds spiking to 35% on Houthi attacks serves as political cover for negotiations that keep crude elevated.
MarketsDefense & SecurityUS Policy & Power
▾

Kevin Warsh just accidentally handed Trump the inflation narrative he needs. Houthi attacks spiked crude to $100, which triggered hike odds to spike from 11% to 35%, which gives Warsh political justification to signal toughness on inflation. But here's the move: if the Fed actually hikes even once on this oil shock, shipping and energy costs stay sticky above $80/barrel, inflation persists, and Warsh can credibly claim the Fed is "fighting the good fight." That same persistence means Trump can negotiate a Houthi ceasefire (or claim to) without looking weak on inflation, because inflation already has an external scapegoat. The pause in attacks by July 27 was suspiciously convenient—it handed crude back to $82 just as the Fed was deciding. Watch if Trump uses the board room at the September Xi meeting to frame an "Iran de-escalation" as mutually beneficial oil-price stability. If Warsh hikes on Wednesday, Trump has negotiating room. If Warsh holds, he looks dovish and crude volatility spikes again, forcing a later hike. Either way, Trump wins the diplomatic timeline.

Frontier Tech / AIChinaMarkets
▾

Nvidia moved from selling chips to betting on outcomes it doesn't control.

Big Tech Capex Is Trapped in a Coordination Problem

Alphabet's negative free cash flow isn't failure; it's evidence every hyperscaler is spending not for ROI but to avoid losing the race.
Applied Tech & IndustryMarketsFrontier Tech / AI
▾

Alphabet dropped 7% after reporting negative $5.9 billion free cash flow for the first time since its 2004 IPO, while announcing capex is rising to $205 billion and 2027 will go "significantly" higher. Morgan Stanley now estimates Alphabet alone could be at $375 billion annually. But here's the dark insight: Alphabet probably knows this capex doesn't independently pencil out. The company is trapped in a prisoner's dilemma with Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon. Each one must spend because if they don't, competitors will seize the AI race, even if the ROI never materializes. Alphabet's negative FCF is a feature of the trap, not a bug. The moment any major player signals they're slowing capex—which a Fed hike or a forced recession could trigger—the entire game inverts. Everyone cuts simultaneously. When that happens, these valuations reset hard because investors will realize the capex was coerced competition, not rational investment. Watch the 2027 capex guidance closely this week. If Microsoft, Meta, Apple, or Amazon signal meaningful slowdowns on capital plans, the entire $200+ billion annual spend thesis collapses in a cascade. That's a 20-30% correction in Big Tech within six months.

China's Open-Weight Model Release Is Asymmetric Geopolitical Warfare

Kimi K3 free download creates a US developer constituency dependent on Chinese models just as Xi prepares his September Washington visit.
ChinaFrontier Tech / AIGeopolitics
▾

Moonshot AI released Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter frontier model, as a free download on July 27—the same week Nvidia's chip-smuggling problems in Taiwan and Singapore are being prosecuted and Xi Jinping's September 24 Washington visit is being prepped. This is not coincidental. China is executing a long-horizon play: release frontier-scale models for free, create a constituency of American developers who depend on Chinese models, build geopolitical leverage, then use that leverage during "strategic competition" negotiations at the Xi-Trump meeting. Meanwhile, the US AI industry is internally fractured—Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang publicly backed open-source development, while Anthropic (Claude Opus 5) is doubling down on proprietary models. The US has no unified policy response because it can't agree on strategy. By September, millions of American developers will have Kimi K3 in production environments. Revoking or blocking access becomes politically costly. Rubio's call for de-escalation on Houthi attacks and the Xi state visit prep are the overture; AI cooperation will be the centerpiece. The real leverage has already shifted: Kimi adoption is the currency, not chip sales or export controls. By 2027, watch whether US AI firms face implicit pressure to open-source more aggressively as a condition of market access in China, and whether Moonshot's free model strategy forces Anthropic and OpenAI into a race-to-the-bottom on pricing or openness.
