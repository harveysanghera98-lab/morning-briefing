URGENT AI Regulation & Governance

1,178 AI Employees Sign 'Pacing the Frontier' Letter, Urging Washington to Build Slowdown Tools

In an unprecedented show of internal dissent, over a thousand engineers and researchers at the biggest frontier labs have jointly asked the US government to develop the technical and governance infrastructure for a verifiable, coordinated AI slowdown — if and when models outpace human oversight.
▾

On July 28–29, the 'Pacing the Frontier' open letter published signatures from 1,178 employees at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta. The letter does not call for an immediate pause but asks Washington to help construct the infrastructure that would make a verifiable, coordinated slowdown possible if AI systems begin compounding faster than oversight mechanisms can handle. Signatories include OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki, Anthropic co-founders Jack Clark and Jared Kaplan, Meta chief scientist Shengjia Zhao, and Google DeepMind's Anca Dragan — making this perhaps the most credentialed collective safety statement the industry has ever produced. Both OpenAI and Anthropic endorsed the letter at the corporate level within hours of publication. Meta's chief scientist signed individually even as CEO Mark Zuckerberg published a same-week Wall Street Journal essay arguing the opposite direction on open access — a notable internal tension at Meta. The divergence between Zuckerberg's public posture and his own chief scientist's signature is the most revealing fault line to watch. The letter lands at a moment when Congress is already debating the bipartisan Great American AI Act of 2026 and as the Trump administration's deregulatory National AI Legislative Framework is under active challenge from states. Whether the White House, currently committed to an 'innovation-first' posture, engages with the letter's specific asks — monitoring infrastructure, international frameworks, verifiability research — or dismisses it as labs protecting incumbency will define the next phase of AI governance negotiations.

HIGH Frontier Tech / AI

Anthropic's Claude Mythos Cracks Post-Quantum Crypto; AI Security Alarm Deepens

An unreleased Anthropic model independently identified critical weaknesses in two cryptographic systems — including a NIST post-quantum candidate — in under 60 hours, advances that had eluded human cryptographers for years.
▾

Anthropic's unreleased Claude Mythos Preview model attacked two cryptographic systems in a research context, producing results that alarmed the cryptography community. Within roughly 60 hours, the model halved the effective key strength of HAWK, a post-quantum digital signature algorithm under active NIST standardization consideration. It also independently invented a technique called the 'Möbius Bridge' that accelerates attacks on 7-round AES by 200–800 times. Neither result breaks production deployments — HAWK has not been deployed, and full-round AES remains secure — but both represent state-of-the-art advances that human experts had not achieved. The find compounds an already fraught security week: OpenAI disclosed that a prior breach involved four stolen employee accounts, and Hugging Face detected and contained a separate infrastructure intrusion before OpenAI notified the company. The Five Eyes intelligence alliance had already warned this month that frontier AI will 'fundamentally transform' offensive cyber capabilities and that the timeline is 'not years, it is months.' The cryptography results in particular will accelerate pressure on NIST to finalize and diversify its post-quantum portfolio, and are likely to surface in congressional hearings on the Great American AI Act. The convergence of AI-assisted cryptanalysis, ongoing lab breaches, and the Pacing the Frontier letter forms a coherent signal: the safety and security risks of frontier models are no longer speculative.

China Inc. Launches $10B Buyback Wave to Stem Equity Rout

Chinese listed companies have announced nearly 67.6 billion yuan in share repurchases so far in July — the most since the April 2025 tariff shock — signaling Beijing-orchestrated market stabilization as economic confidence falters.
▾

Chinese companies have announced 67.6 billion yuan ($9.99 billion) in share buybacks in July 2026, approaching levels last seen during the April 2025 tariff-driven market rout, according to Bloomberg citing exchange data. The scale and speed of the buyback wave bears hallmarks of coordinated state guidance, with Beijing using corporate balance sheets as a stabilization tool. The move comes as China's equity markets face a confluence of pressures: domestic demand remains soft, property sector stress lingers, and Chinese imports of oil have been slashed by an estimated 4–5 million barrels per day — a signal of subdued industrial activity that is simultaneously keeping global oil markets from pricing in full Middle East risk. On the technology front, US-China AI rivalry is intensifying in Asia, with Washington pushing allies to adopt American AI stacks while Chinese alternatives — led by models like Kimi K3 — compete on price. The Wall Street Journal has reported on Zuckerberg's open-access essay as a counterpoint to the US government's more restrictive posture. Analysts at RBC Capital Markets note that oil is currently a 'broken barometer' for Middle East risk because Chinese demand suppression is masking what would otherwise be a more severe price spike. Watch for any official PBOC or CSRC commentary endorsing or directing the buyback surge, and for Q2 GDP revisions due later this week.

US VC Hits Record $412.7B in H1 2026, But 81% Concentrated in $100M+ Mega-Rounds

The venture market is setting all-time records in headline dollars while becoming structurally more concentrated, with 16 megafunds capturing nearly 70% of LP capital and AI infrastructure commanding the largest checks.
▾

US venture investment reached $412.7 billion in H1 2026, already surpassing any previous full-year total, according to PitchBook and NVCA data cited by Axios. But the distribution is extreme: more than 81% of dollars went into rounds of $100 million or more, and 16 megafunds raised nearly 70% of the $72.4 billion in VC fund formation during the period, per the Wall Street Journal. The theme driving the largest checks is AI infrastructure — compute, chips, cooling, agent platforms, and domain-specific deployment — rather than consumer apps. Emblematic deals this month include Saudi Aramco Ventures leading an $800 million Series C in Together AI (valuing it at $8.3 billion), SambaNova raising $1 billion for AI chips backed by Intel and General Atlantic, and Nvidia-backed ChipAgents raising $60 million to automate semiconductor design. Separately, Etched launched at a $5 billion valuation with $1 billion in signed inference-chip contracts. The concentration dynamic has a shadow side: seed and Series A activity remains depressed, limiting the pipeline of next-generation companies. Limited partners remain cautious on liquidity, with the IPO window open only episodically. The week ahead may bring additional mega-round announcements as Q3 gets underway and AI labs continue to expand compute commitments.

When Jakub Pachocki, Jack Clark, and Jared Kaplan sign a letter asking Washington to build governance infrastructure for verifiable slowdown, they are not calling for constraint—they are calling for the regulatory theater that permits them to keep spending billions on megacompute without political backlash. The letter is a collective bet that Washington will build infrastructure without actual slowdown, and the labs will write the verification rules.
