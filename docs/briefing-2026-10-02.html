URGENT Geopolitics

Trump rejects Iran's Hormuz reopening plan; Brent near $108

With the US-Israel war on Iran past seven months, the Strait of Hormuz stays effectively shut and oil is the main transmission channel to inflation and markets.
▾

Al Jazeera reported that Trump rejected Tehran's latest proposal, announced at the UN General Assembly on Friday Sept 25. Under it, the US would release frozen Iranian funds, lift sanctions and end its naval blockade of Iranian ports. In exchange, Iran would reopen the strait and return to nuclear talks within a week. Brent rose more than 3 percent on Monday Sept 28 to nearly $108 a barrel.

Before the war began in late February, roughly one-fifth of global oil supply passed through Hormuz. Commercial traffic has fallen sharply since then because of repeated attacks on ships that have been blamed on Iran or allied groups. Overnight-brief and live-blog aggregators for Oct 1 described a tanker hit by an unidentified projectile in the strait, which caused a fire. They also described three US ships carrying more than 7,000 sailors and 2,000 Marines heading to the region. I could not confirm either item against a primary outlet.

What to watch: whether Washington and Tehran return to any back-channel talks, further tanker incidents, and how the oil price feeds into September inflation data and the Fed's next decision. Treasury yields are already near multi-decade highs.

Jobs report day with the Fed in a hiking bias

Today's payrolls and the September CPI will shape whether the Fed hikes again, with oil near $108 and yields near 24-year highs.
▾

Search snippets put consensus for today's nonfarm payrolls at roughly +84,000 with unemployment steady at 4.1%. They also put wage growth at about 0.3% m/m. CNBC also reported that Trump has been pressuring Fed Chair Warsh as a hike loomed. The policy debate is now about tightening, not cutting. Search snippets said Wednesday's close was marginally higher (S&P +0.19%) as Q4 began, with the 10-year just under 24-year highs.

Watch the payrolls print against consensus, wage growth, and the next CPI release. A hot number would raise hike odds for the next FOMC meeting, and a weak one would complicate the case for hiking. Oil remains the main upside risk to inflation.

HIGH US Policy & Power

Funding stopgap runs to Dec 11, so shutdown risk is off for now

A bipartisan continuing resolution keeps the government open into December and sets up a year-end appropriations fight.
▾

Breaking Defense reported that the House passed a continuing resolution 370-48 that funds the government through December 11, 2026. It holds Pentagon funding at FY2026 levels. It excluded some special requests, including $1 billion for a Trump-class battleship and certain munitions procurement authority. The article said the Senate had passed the bill earlier through a bipartisan agreement, and that it awaited the president's signature. The date details in that report look inconsistent, so check the status.

The backdrop is a record 43-day shutdown in fall 2025 and a four-day lapse in January 2026. A clean extension lowers near-term disruption risk for contractors and agencies. It also pushes the defense budget and full-year appropriations fight into the December window.

Watch for the signing, any Iran-war supplemental requests, and the pre-midterm politics of the December deadline.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Trump's Iran Gambit Backfires Into Stagflation

Warsh will hike despite political pressure as Hormuz closure locks in oil-driven inflation
GeopoliticsMarketsUS Policy & Power
▾

Trump's rejection of Iran's Hormuz reopening plan is being sold as a hardline move, but it's setting up an economic trap. With Brent at $108 and the strait effectively closed since late February, oil is now the primary transmission channel for inflation into Q4 2026. Trump has been openly pressuring Fed Chair Warsh to avoid rate hikes, but that political leverage evaporates if energy prices stay elevated. Warsh will face a choice: follow Trump's signals and lose credibility on inflation fighting, or hike and humiliate the president publicly. The market has already priced in hike fears (2-year yield at January 2025 highs). If Hormuz-related tanker attacks accelerate or Tehran escalates, oil breaks $110, and Warsh's hand is completely forced. Trump loses both the inflation fight and his ability to manage the Fed chairman.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Pentagon's Defense Budget Freeze vs. Silicon Valley's AI Blitz

Military compute infrastructure is now structurally behind private-sector deployments
US Policy & PowerFrontier Tech / AIDefense & Security
▾

The continuing resolution holds Pentagon spending at FY2026 levels through December, freezing modernization spending while OpenAI and Nvidia commit $100 billion to 10 gigawatts of Vera Rubin AI compute deployed by H2 2026. This is not a temporary gap—it's a structural divergence. By the time the December appropriations fight happens, Vera Rubin's first gigawatt will be operational in Silicon Valley, while CENTCOM and Space Force are still planning AI upgrades on 2024 budgets. Three US ships carrying over 7,000 sailors and 2,000 Marines are heading to the Middle East as the Hormuz conflict escalates, and they are sailing into a theater where their ISR and targeting algorithms are generations behind what OpenAI is deploying in commercial cloud infrastructure. The Pentagon will need emergency supplementals in early 2027 just to close this gap.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Energy Price Shock Threatens AI Buildout Margins

Vera Rubin deployments arrive into a power-crisis environment, crushing the unit economics Nvidia promised
EnergyFrontier Tech / AIMarkets
▾

Vera Rubin's first gigawatt of Nvidia systems is scheduled to go live in H2 2026, but the timing is terrible. Brent is at $108, Ukrainian power infrastructure is under sustained Russian attack, and European energy capacity is constrained. Data center power costs are about to spike, and they are a direct line-item on OpenAI's margin sheet. Nvidia's $100 billion progressive investment in the 10 GW buildout assumes stable power costs and grid access—assumptions that look fragile in a world where the Strait of Hormuz is closed and a seven-month US-Iran war shows no off-ramps. If Firmus's Malaysian capacity and other Vera Rubin nodes come online at $120+ per barrel oil, the unit economics of large-scale AI training invert. Investors will start asking whether Nvidia's commitment is viable, and whether the entire buildout gets stepped back in Q1 2027.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Weak Jobs, Sticky Wages: Stagflation Setup Is Already In

Today's payrolls will show labor slack, but wage growth won't break—the worst policy combination imaginable
Labor & WorkforceMarketsUS Policy & Power
▾

Today's consensus is for +84,000 nonfarm payrolls with unemployment steady at 4.1%—a clear sign hiring is sputtering. Wage growth expectations are 0.3% month-over-month, or about 3.6% annualized, which is not rolling over. That combination is stagflation textbook: labor market weakness plus sticky inflation. Trump's pressure on Warsh to avoid hikes becomes irrelevant because the Fed faces an inflation problem it cannot cut its way out of and a growth problem it cannot hike its way out of either. The Treasury market has already front-run this scenario—10-year yields at 24-year highs despite recession risk. The real repricing happens after October's jobs and CPI reports lock in stagflation expectations. By December, the FOMC is trapped between political pressure and economic reality, and monetary policy loses its power to solve this problem.
