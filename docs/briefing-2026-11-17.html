URGENT Frontier Tech / AI

Nvidia reports first earnings since Jensen Huang's resignation

Nvidia reports after the close today with interim CEO Colette Kress in charge, and the AI trade is already shaky.
▾

Nvidia reports fiscal Q3 2027 results after the bell today. The report is its first since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on Nov. 11. Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO. Guidance was about $108 billion in revenue, up from $96.2 billion last quarter. Consensus is about $109 billion and EPS of $2.47.

The numbers may matter less than the governance questions. Kress is expected to face questions on Huang's departure, a reported board investigation, and dealings with firms accused of violating US export controls. Shares were around $156 after falling about 4% in the latest session. Coverage describes Huang's exit as the trigger for a broad risk-asset sell-off.

Monday's session was rough. The S&P 500 fell about 1.9% and the Nasdaq about 2.5%, and the S&P is down roughly 2.1% since the end of October. The sources I found are previews and market commentary, not original reporting from the FT, WSJ or Reuters, so views across outlets could not be compared. Watch the Q4 guidance, any CEO succession timeline, and comments on export-control exposure.

HIGH Applied Tech & Industry

Anthropic IPO set to price this week, up to $82B total raise

One of the largest tech listings ever lands in a falling AI market, with huge compute commitments attached.
▾

Anthropic is set to debut under the ticker ANT, with trading implied for Thursday, Nov. 19. It plans to sell 825 million shares at $70-$78, raising up to $64 billion. A separate $26 billion private placement goes to investors including Google, Nvidia and AMD. The total raise is about $82 billion, which would bring its war chest to roughly $212.7 billion.

Revenue was $33.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026, versus $2.5 billion a year earlier. The GAAP net loss was $104 billion, mostly from a non-cash charge tied to revaluing convertible notes and other instruments. Anthropic also has roughly $900 billion in compute and infrastructure commitments, much of it non-cancelable. If growth falls short, it could struggle to meet them.

The timing is awkward. Nvidia's earnings and Huang's exit have unsettled AI stocks just ahead of the pricing. Anthropic's long-running dispute with the Pentagon over its supply-chain-risk designation is also a risk factor. I could not find the latest status of that case. Watch for the final price range and first-day trading.

HIGH Markets

Fed minutes loom as rate-hike debate heats up

Two Fed dissenters wanted a hike in October, and hot inflation data has since reinforced that case.
▾

The Fed holds the minutes of its Oct. 27-28 meeting for release Wednesday at 2 p.m. ET. It held rates at 3.75%-4.00%. Governors Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point hike. Since then, October CPI came in hotter than expected and jobs data looked better than expected.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh spoke Monday at a San Francisco conference. Today's October retail sales are expected to show no monthly growth after September's 0.8% gain. A strong print would strengthen the case for another hike. Weakness would ease the pressure.

I did not find the original Reuters, FT or WSJ coverage. Earlier Reuters reporting from late October put the market odds of a December hike at about 85%. Oil was also elevated then, with Brent near $114 on Oct. 27. Watch how many officials the minutes show were open to a hike.

Bitcoin near $64,700 as Russia opens retail crypto access

Crypto is selling off alongside AI stocks, while Russia's new retail rules take effect today.
▾

Bitcoin was around $64,700 at CoinDesk's publication. One headline there warned it could fall below $60,000 if Nvidia's results deepen the sell-off. It has not benefited from the AI stock slump.

Russia's crypto-access ordinance takes effect today. It lets non-qualified investors buy bitcoin, ether and USDT up to an annual limit per intermediary. Russian exchanges may begin organized trading from Nov. 22 if the Bank of Russia approves their rules. Strong retail sales would raise the odds of a Fed hike, which would weigh on crypto.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

The Anthropic IPO Is a Pentagon Settlement With a Ticker

Speculative: the supply-chain-risk fight ends in a government-blessed deal that lifts the IPO to the top of its range.
Applied Tech & IndustryDefense & SecurityUS Policy & Power
▾

Speculative: the market is treating Anthropic's Pentagon supply-chain-risk designation as a footnote in the S-1 risk factors. I think that is backwards. A company raising up to $64 billion in public money, plus a $26 billion private placement from Google, Nvidia and AMD, cannot afford an open-ended federal blacklist on its roster. My guess is the Pentagon case gets quietly settled before or shortly after pricing, with the designation narrowed or lifted in exchange for tighter access controls and classified-workload commitments. Anthropic gets the clean story it needs; the administration gets a visible win on AI security and a 'domestic champion' headline.

The second-order call: the price range gets pushed to the top, or the deal is upsized, if that settlement lands in the next two weeks. Investors pricing a company with roughly $900 billion in non-cancelable compute commitments will pay for political de-risking more than for the revenue line. Revenue went from $2.5 billion to $33.2 billion year over year, but the $104 billion GAAP loss is mostly non-cash and will get ignored by anyone who wants the ticker to work.

The contrarian part: if the settlement does not come, the IPO still prices, but the first-day trade looks weaker than the bulls expect, because the Pentagon overhang becomes a permanent discount on any federal contract revenue. Either way, the Defense & Security side of this story is the real pricing input, not the consumer AI hype.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Hike Odds, Bitcoin Below 60K, and Russia's Retail Crypto Valve

Speculative: tightening plus Russian retail access turns crypto into a capital-flight and sanctions-adjacent story, not a risk-on one.
MarketsDigital FinanceGeopolitics
▾

Speculative: Bitcoin near $64,700 is being sold with AI stocks, which is the wrong reflex for an asset that is supposed to hedge monetary debasement. With Cook and Hammack already dissenting for a hike in October and October CPI running hot, I expect the minutes Wednesday to show a larger group open to tightening. Chair Kevin Warsh has a choice between credibility and a growth scare, and the market is pricing that choice into every high-beta asset. Bitcoin is the first casualty because it is the most levered expression of 'cheap money forever.' My call: BTC trades below $60,000 before the December meeting if the retail sales print is strong.

Now the Russia angle. Russia's ordinance lets non-qualified investors buy bitcoin, ether and USDT up to an annual limit per intermediary, with organized exchange trading possibly starting Nov. 22. Speculatively, this is less a consumer product than a dollar-stablecoin and crypto pipeline for a sanctioned economy facing tight Western finance and a strong-dollar rate environment. Tether's USDT being explicitly named is the tell. Expect Western compliance teams and Treasury's sanctions staff to treat intermediaries that onboard Russian retail flows as the next enforcement target, with the Bank of Russia's approval of exchange rules as the trigger to watch.

The bold version: a Fed hike in December plus a Russian retail crypto channel produces a two-sided squeeze where crypto falls in price in the West and rises in volume in Russia. The price story and the sanctions story get told separately, and nobody connects them until a stablecoin freeze makes the headlines.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Nvidia's Interim CEO Inherits a Circular-Revenue Problem

Speculative: Kress's first big decision is how to sell into China and the Anthropic buildout without making the export-control probe worse.
Frontier Tech / AIChinaAI Regulation & Governance
▾

Speculative: the Nvidia print is about governance, but the real question is exposure. Colette Kress is an interim CEO with a reported board investigation hanging over her and dealings with firms accused of export-control violations under review. A sensible interim CEO in that position does not make a bold move. I think she does the opposite: she leans on a conservative guide, avoids any new China-specific announcements, and lets the investigation play out quietly. Anyone expecting Nvidia to publicly hint at a new China chip SKU this quarter is likely to be disappointed, and that disappointment is the actual catalyst.

The overlap that nobody on the sell side wants to say out loud: Anthropic is raising capital from Nvidia and AMD while also holding roughly $900 billion in compute commitments that flow largely to hardware suppliers. If a meaningful share of Nvidia's demand is coming from companies that Nvidia is also investing in, the revenue quality question becomes a governance question. Speculatively, the first analyst to ask Kress about circular financing on the call gets a non-answer, and that non-answer becomes the week's headline.

My sharp take: the export-control probe is the thing to watch, not Q4 guidance. If Commerce or the Justice Department signals a broader inquiry into Nvidia's channel partners in Southeast Asia within 90 days, Kress becomes the CEO who presided over the company's biggest legal exposure, and a permanent CEO is named under pressure rather than on a planned timeline.

SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS

Oil at $114 Is the Hidden Hand Behind the AI Capex Bubble

Speculative: energy-driven inflation keeps the Fed hawkish, and that makes Anthropic's $900 billion compute promise a power and credit problem.
EnergyMarketsApplied Tech & Industry
▾

Speculative: almost every AI story this week is being told as an equity story, but the binding constraints are power and the cost of money. Brent was near $114 in late October, and energy costs feed directly into the inflation prints that are pushing the Fed toward a hike. The chain is simple: oil stays high, headline CPI stays hot, Warsh's Fed stays hawkish, and the cost of financing multi-year data center buildouts rises at exactly the moment those buildouts are being contracted. Speculatively, that is the real reason the AI trade is shaky, and Nvidia's earnings are a symptom, not the cause.

Anthropic is the cleanest test case. Its roughly $900 billion in mostly non-cancelable compute and infrastructure commitments are, in practice, long-dated power purchase agreements with a software company stapled on top. If revenue growth slows while rates stay high, the company cannot easily walk away from those commitments, and the first credit-style question about AI infrastructure will be asked about a company that just went public. Revenue of $33.2 billion through nine months is strong, but it is measured against obligations that run for a decade.

The bold call: the first real stress signal in AI infrastructure will come from utilities and power developers signing data center contracts at higher rates, not from the chip companies. Watch for a utility or independent power producer to disclose a renegotiation or a delayed campus tied to a hyperscale or frontier-lab customer in the first half of next year. That is the moment the 'AI is infinite' narrative meets the Fed and the oil market.
